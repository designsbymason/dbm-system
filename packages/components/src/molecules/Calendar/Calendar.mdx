<VisuallyHidden asChild><h2>Intro</h2></VisuallyHidden>

<ComponentHeader title="Calendar" tier="Molecule" />

A month of days to choose a date, or a range of dates, from: a heading with previous and next month buttons above a
grid of seven columns — one for each day of the week — and six rows. The grid always holds the same 42 days, so it
keeps one height whichever month it shows and nothing below it moves as you page through the year. Use it on its
own — a booking view, a schedule, a date filter — or as the grid inside a date field.

A date is the string `"YYYY-MM-DD"`, never a JavaScript `Date`: a day on a calendar has no time of day and no
timezone, and a `Date` carries both. So the value is plain text that compares, sorts and submits exactly as it is.
`value` and `onValueChange` (or an uncontrolled `defaultValue`) hold the choice; with `mode="range"` the value is a
`[start, end]` pair, with `mode="multiple"` a list of any number of separate dates, and with `mode="week"` the date a chosen
week starts on. The month on show is `"YYYY-MM"`, held by `month` and `onMonthChange` (or `defaultMonth`).
`min`, `max` and `isDateDisabled` rule dates out, `weekStartsOn` sets the first column, and `showOutsideDays` fills the
first and last weeks with the neighbouring months' days.

A mouse can drag across days to choose a range, a finger can swipe sideways to turn the month, a choice is announced to
screen readers, `name` makes the calendar submit with a form, and `showWeekNumbers`, `fixedWeeks` and `animated` shape
the grid. Several months can sit side by side (`numberOfMonths`), the month and year can be two select fields for a far-off date
(`captionLayout="dropdown"`), a footer can offer **Today** and **Clear** buttons and your own content, a day can carry a
marker (`getMarker`), and a range can be limited in length or kept from running over an unavailable date.

The grid is one tab stop and is driven by the keyboard — arrows by the day and the week, Home and End along the week,
Page Up and Page Down by the month — and the month you move to is announced to screen readers.

## Variants

### All sizes

A day is a square as tall as an `IconButton` of the same size, so the grid lines up with the buttons around it, and the
calendar is seven of them wide.

<Canvas of={CalendarStories.Sizes} />

### Choosing a range

With `mode="range"` the first choice is the start of the range and the second the end; a third starts again, and a
choice before the start moves the start. While the end is still to be chosen, the range is previewed up to the day the
pointer or the keyboard focus is on. With a mouse you can also press on the first day and drag to the last: the range is
drawn as you go and chosen when you let go (by the same limits as choosing its end). The strip behind a range is rounded
where it meets the end of a row.

<Canvas of={CalendarStories.Range} />

### Rounded

`rounded` draws the days and both month buttons round: a circle for each day, the chosen days, today's ring and the
focus ring. The strip behind a range keeps its straight edges, so it still reads as one run.

<Canvas of={CalendarStories.Rounded} />

### With a key

`showLegend` adds a key below the calendar that says what each look means — the ring around today, the solid fill of a
chosen date (or the ends of a range), the soft fill between them, and the dimmed look of an unavailable date. It lists
only the looks that are on screen: with nothing chosen there is no "Selected" entry, a single date has no range entry,
and a calendar with no rules has no "Unavailable" one, so the key comes and goes with the choice. (The prop could not
be called `key`, which React keeps for itself.) The words are in `labels`: `legendToday`, `legendSelected`,
`legendRange` and `legendUnavailable`, and `legend` names the list.

<Canvas of={CalendarStories.Legend} />

### Month and year fields

`captionLayout="dropdown"` swaps the heading for a month field and a year field, so a far-off date is one choice away
instead of many presses of the month buttons. `yearRange` sets the years offered (the hundred before this year and the
twenty after it, left out), `min` and `max` narrow it further and rule out the months they leave no day in, and the year on
show is always offered. The month field's closed button shows a short name ("Oct", from `labels.monthsShort`) so the
longest months ("September") never push its arrow out of the button; the list it opens shows the full names. With several
months on show only the first gets the fields.

<Canvas of={CalendarStories.Dropdowns} />

### Year and month views

`captionLayout="views"` makes the heading a button. Choosing it swaps the days for a grid of the year's twelve months;
choosing the year there swaps that for a grid of years, twelve to a page. Choosing a year goes to its months, and a month goes
back to the days; `Escape` closes a grid. Each grid is as tall as the days it replaces, so nothing below the calendar moves.
Use it when the fields of `captionLayout="dropdown"` feel too heavy, or where a drill-down is what people expect. With more
than one month on show it is the plain heading.

<Canvas of={CalendarStories.Views} />

### Two months

`numberOfMonths` puts up to four months side by side — two is the usual choice for a range that may cross a month.
The previous and next buttons move the first month on show by one, and the keyboard turns the page only when focus
leaves the months on show. In a container too narrow for them the months wrap onto more rows. The neighbouring
months' days are not drawn, as they would appear twice.

<Canvas of={CalendarStories.TwoMonths} />

### Today, Clear and a footer of your own

`showTodayButton` shows today's month and chooses today when it can be chosen; `clearable` empties the choice. Neither
button leaves the footer or loses keyboard focus when it can't be used — it is `aria-disabled` instead — so pressing
Clear on the last chosen date leaves focus on Clear. `footer` is your own content after them: a note, a link, a row of
presets.

<Canvas of={CalendarStories.Footer} />

### Markers on days

`getMarker` is asked for each day drawn and can return a dot in a tone, a `label` that is added to the day's accessible name,
or your own `content` (a price, a count) for the slot under the number. A marked day with only a dot keeps its number
centred; once any day brings content of its own, every number moves to the top of its day so they stay in line along a row.
Content needs room, so use it from size `lg`.

<Canvas of={CalendarStories.Markers} />

### Range limits

`minRangeDays` and `maxRangeDays` count both ends (the 5th to the 7th is 3 days). While the end is being chosen, the
dates that would make the range too short or too long are unavailable. `rangeSpansUnavailable={false}` also rules out
every date after the first unavailable one, for a booking that can't step over a booked night. A date before the
start still restarts the range.

<Canvas of={CalendarStories.RangeLimits} />

### A whole week

With `mode="week"` any day chooses its whole row. The value is the date the week starts on (by `weekStartsOn`); a date
that isn't a week's first day is read as the week it is in. The week under the pointer is drawn lightly before it is
chosen, and a week can be chosen by any of its available days — for a rule about whole weeks, write it in `isDateDisabled`.

<Canvas of={CalendarStories.Week} />

### Several dates

With `mode="multiple"` choosing a date adds it and choosing it again takes it away. The value is a list, reported in date
order; `maxSelected` stops further dates once that many are chosen (the chosen ones stay free, so one can be taken away).
Each change is announced as a count — "2 dates selected".

<Canvas of={CalendarStories.Multiple} />

### Week numbers

`showWeekNumbers` adds a column of ISO 8601 week numbers before the days, taken from each row's Thursday. They are not
interactive and are named ("Week 41") for screen readers.

<Canvas of={CalendarStories.WeekNumbers} />

### Only the rows a month needs

A month is drawn as six rows by default, so the grid keeps one height whichever month it shows and nothing below moves.
`fixedWeeks={false}` draws only the rows the month needs — four to six — and what is below the calendar moves as the month
changes.

<Canvas of={CalendarStories.FlexibleWeeks} />

### In a form

`name` makes the calendar submit with a surrounding `<form>`, through hidden inputs: a single date as `name`, a range as two
values under `name[]` (start, then end), and several dates as one `name[]` each. A disabled calendar submits nothing.

<Canvas of={CalendarStories.InAForm} />

### In another language

Every name the calendar writes — the month and weekday names, the buttons, the heading, the name of each day — is in
the `labels` object, with English defaults. Translating `months` and `weekdays` is enough to translate the heading
and the day names built from them.

### Numbers in your own locale

`formatNumber` writes each day and the year the way a language or region does — here Arabic-Indic digits, through the
browser's own `Intl.NumberFormat`. The default accessible names use it too, so what a day shows is always inside its
name. If you write your own `labels.day` or `labels.monthYear`, they are given the plain numbers, so write them with the
same function.

## Usage guidelines

<div className="dbm-do-dont-grid">
<Callout tone="success" title="Do">

- Use `Calendar` when the person benefits from seeing the dates around the one they choose — a booking, a
  schedule, a date a few weeks or months away. For a date they can simply type, a field is faster.
- Keep the date as the `"YYYY-MM-DD"` string all the way through your app and convert only when you must show it
  (`Intl.DateTimeFormat` on a `Date` made from it). Comparing the strings with `<` and `>` is correct.
- Set `weekStartsOn` for your audience: 1 (Monday) for most of Europe, 0 (Sunday) in the United States, 6 (Saturday)
  in much of the Middle East. It is never read from the browser, so the page doesn't change under a reader.
- Rule dates out with `min`, `max` and `isDateDisabled` rather than hiding them: the grid keeps its shape, and a
  person can see that the day exists but isn't available.
- Leave `today` out in the app: the calendar reads the real date once it has mounted. Pass it only in tests and
  screenshots, so the marked day doesn't change with the date they run.
- Translate the text with `labels`, and pass `dir="rtl"` for a right-to-left language, or wrap the app in a `DirectionProvider`; it is not read from the page.
- Give each calendar on a page its own `aria-label` ("Departure", "Return") when there is more than one.

</Callout>
<Callout tone="danger" title="Don't">

- Don't pass a `Date`, a timestamp, or a differently shaped string. The value is `"YYYY-MM-DD"`; anything else reads
  as no date and warns in development.
- Don't hand it a `value` and forget `onValueChange`. A controlled calendar that never hears the change looks frozen.
  Use `defaultValue` if you don't need to know the date as it changes.
- Don't give both `value` and `defaultValue`, or both `month` and `defaultMonth`. The controlled one wins, and the
  calendar warns in development.
- Don't use `isDateDisabled` for a rule that is slow to compute: it is called for each of the 42 days on every render.
- Don't use it to choose a time as well. It chooses a day; pair it with a `TimePicker` for a time of day.
- Don't put it on `bg.canvas`: the neighbouring months' days use the quietest text colour, which doesn't reach the
  4.5:1 floor on the canvas. The ordinary page background is what it is checked on.
- Don't rely on the month buttons for a far-off date (a birth date): use `captionLayout="dropdown"`, or a field you can
  type into.
- Don't put a lot into a marker's `content`: it sits under the number in a slot as high as the text, and it is hidden from
  assistive technology, so give it a short `label`.
- Don't expect a swipe to choose a range on a touch screen: it turns the month. Choose with two taps.
- Don't turn `fixedWeeks` off in a layout where a button or a note sits directly under the calendar: it will move.
- Don't use `rangeSpansUnavailable={false}` with a rule that makes many dates unavailable (every weekend): a range could
  then never run over one.

</Callout>
</div>

## Best practices

- A calendar is seven days wide, so `md` is 294px wide. On a narrower container it shrinks to fit (the days stop being
  exact squares), but never below seven 24px days (168px), the smallest target WCAG allows: narrower than that it
  overflows its container (and scrolls, if the container does) rather than squeezing the days into each other. Use
  `sm` or `xs` where space is tight.
- Add `showLegend` where a person meets the calendar for the first time and has to read the fills — a booking flow, a
  range picker — and leave it off for a familiar date field. Say what a fill *means* in your own words in `labels`
  when the context needs it ("Your stay", "Fully booked").
- Use `rounded` for a softer look that matches rounded buttons elsewhere on the page; leave it off beside square-cornered
  fields.
- In `range` mode, tell the person what to do next. After the first choice the value is `[start, ""]` — show "choose
  the last day" beside it, as the range story does — and treat a range with an empty end as not finished.
- A range of one day is allowed: choosing the start again as the end gives `[date, date]`.
- Choose between the two ways of jumping far: `captionLayout="dropdown"` is one choice per field and suits a date a person
  knows (a birth year); `"views"` narrows down year, month, day and suits browsing. They are not combined.
- Offer `mode="week"` for timesheets, weekly reports and anything that is a week rather than a day, and set `weekStartsOn`
  to where your weeks start.
- Offer `mode="multiple"` for days off, availability, or any set of dates that aren't one run, and `maxSelected` when only
  so many make sense.
- Leave `fixedWeeks` on unless something below the calendar is allowed to jump as the month changes.
- Dragging is a mouse shortcut, never the only way: a click on the first day and a click on the last does the same, and the
  keyboard has Enter. On a touch screen a tap does it, and a swipe turns the month.
- Use two months for a range that is likely to cross a month, and one for a single date. Remember `month` is then the
  first month on show.
- A marker is for a fact about a day that the person can't see otherwise — an event, a booking, a price. Give it a `label`
  so a screen-reader user hears it, and keep the number of kinds small.
- Keep a controlled `month` when something outside the calendar should follow it (a list of events for the month on
  show), and let it manage the month itself otherwise.
- `min` and `max` also stop the month buttons: the previous button becomes unavailable in the month that holds `min`,
  and the next in the month that holds `max`. They stay focusable, so focus is never lost.
- The dates the arrow keys visit include unavailable ones, on purpose: skipping them would make the keys jump
  unpredictably. They can be focused and named, but not chosen.
- Today's marker is read from the browser's clock once the calendar has mounted, so a server-rendered page and its first
  client render agree. The clock is read once; a page left open past midnight keeps yesterday marked until it is
  reloaded.

## Accessibility

<Callout tone="info" title="A named group around an ARIA date grid, one tab stop, and keys for every move">

- The calendar is a `role="group"` named "Calendar" (translatable, or give your own `aria-label`). Inside is a `table`
  with `role="grid"`, named by the month heading ("October 2026"), with seven column headers and six rows of
  `gridcell`s. Each column header shows the short weekday and is named by the full one ("Mo" / "Monday").
- Each day is a real `<button>` inside its cell, named in full ("Monday, October 5, 2026"), and today is also marked
  `aria-current="date"` and named "…, today". The chosen date, or every date of a range, is `aria-selected="true"` on its
  cell; the ends of a range are named "range start" and "range end". An unavailable date's cell is `aria-disabled`.
- Only one day is in the tab order — the one that last had focus, else the chosen date, else today, else the 1st — so
  `Tab` goes to the grid in one step, past the previous and next month buttons, and leaves it in one. Arrow keys move
  within it (roving focus).
- The previous and next month buttons are `aria-disabled`, not natively disabled, when a limit is reached: pressing one
  into its last month leaves focus where it was instead of dropping it to the top of the page.
- When the month changes, the new month ("November 2026") is announced politely through a visually hidden status
  region that is in the page from the start — not the first month shown. Turn it off with `announce={false}`.
- Disabled and read-only are conveyed (`aria-disabled` and `aria-readonly` on the grid) and keep the keyboard working:
  a read-only calendar can still be moved through.
- Contrast: the chosen day's label on its fill measures at least 6.08:1, the day numbers at least 9.66:1 on the plain page
  and the range strip, and the weekday headings at least 6.88:1, in every theme; the neighbouring months' days are
  4.70:1 on the page (and step up to the secondary text on a tinted fill, where the quietest text would fall under
  4.5:1). Today's ring is at least 6.08:1 and the focus ring at least 4.16:1 against the page.
- The year and month views (`captionLayout="views"`) are two more grids, each named for what it holds ("Months of 2026",
  "2017 – 2028"), with one tab stop and roving focus: arrows move by one and by three, Home and End go to the ends of a row,
  Page Up and Page Down turn the year (or the page of years), Enter and Space choose, and Escape closes the grid and returns
  focus to the heading button. The heading button is named for what it opens ("October 2026, choose a month"). Opening a grid
  moves focus into it and says what opened. Months and years that `min` and `max` leave nothing in are `aria-disabled`.
- In week mode every day of the chosen week is `aria-selected`, and the week is announced when it is chosen ("Week Sunday,
  October 11, 2026 to Saturday, October 17, 2026").
- A choice is announced: in range mode "Range start Thursday, October 8, 2026", then "Range Thursday, October 8, 2026 to
  Monday, October 12, 2026"; in multiple mode how many dates are chosen. The words are in `labels`, and `announce={false}`
  turns them off along with the month announcement. A drag is announced when it ends.
- A mouse drag and a touch swipe are shortcuts: everything they do has a keyboard and a tap equivalent, and neither is
  required. Under the reader's reduced-motion preference the month slide is off.
- With `showWeekNumbers` each week's number is a row header named "Week 41"; the column header is named "Week number".
  The numbers are not in the tab order.
- With `captionLayout="dropdown"` the month field's button draws the short name but still holds the full one for assistive
  technology, so a screen reader hears "October", never "Oct"; the short name is hidden from it.
- With `captionLayout="dropdown"` the heading is replaced by two `Select` fields named "Month" and "Year" (translatable);
  the grid keeps its name ("October 2026") through a visually hidden heading. They come between the previous and next
  buttons in the tab order, which then reaches the grid.
- With more than one month, each month is its own named grid, and still all of them are one tab stop together. A key
  that moves onto a day in the next month moves focus there without turning the page; the page turns when focus leaves
  the months on show. The months on show are announced together ("November 2026 – December 2026").
- The Today and Clear buttons are `aria-disabled`, not natively disabled, when they can't be used, so focus is never
  dropped from a button that has just emptied the calendar. A marker's `label` is added to the day's name ("…, 2 events"); a marker that draws text of its own (a price) opens the name with the day's number and that label ("20 $80, Tuesday, October 20, 2026"), so what is on screen is in the name, in order. Each month button in the months grid shows the short name and is named "Jan, January";
  the dot or content itself is hidden from assistive technology. While the end of a range is being chosen, the dates
  the limits rule out are `aria-disabled` like any other unavailable date.
- Every day is a target of at least 30 × 30 CSS pixels at the smallest size, above the 24px minimum, and the calendar
  never gets narrower than seven 24px days, however small its container.
- The key (`showLegend`) is a list named "Key" (translatable). Its sample shapes are decorative and hidden from assistive
  technology, so it reads as plain text — "Today", "Selected", "In range", "Unavailable" — and every look it explains
  is also conveyed in the grid itself by `aria-current`, `aria-selected`, `aria-disabled` and the days' names, never by
  colour alone.
- Forced colours: a chosen day is the system highlight, the strip behind a range becomes two lines, and today an
  outline.

**Keyboard**

| Key | Action |
|---|---|
| `Tab` / `Shift + Tab` | Moves to the previous and next month buttons, then to the grid (its one tab stop), then out. |
| `←` `→` | The previous or next day. Under `dir="rtl"` they follow the picture, so `←` goes forward. |
| `↑` `↓` | The same day of the previous or next week. |
| `Home` / `End` | The first or last day of the week, where `weekStartsOn` puts it. |
| `Page Up` / `Page Down` | The same day of the previous or next month (the last day if it has no such day). |
| `Shift + Page Up` / `Shift + Page Down` | The same day of the previous or next year. |
| `Enter` / `Space` | Chooses the focused day. |

A move onto a day in another month turns the calendar to it. A move never goes before `min` or after `max`.

</Callout>

## Code examples

```tsx
// Controlled: you hold the date, so the rest of the page can react to it
const [date, setDate] = useState("2026-10-06");

<Calendar value={date} onValueChange={setDate} />

// Uncontrolled: it tracks the date itself; you're told when it changes
<Calendar defaultValue="2026-10-06" onValueChange={(date) => track(date)} />

// A range: [start, end], with "" for an end not chosen yet
const [range, setRange] = useState<[string, string]>(["", ""]);

<Calendar mode="range" value={range} onValueChange={setRange} />

// Hold the month too, to show events for the month on show
const [month, setMonth] = useState("2026-10");

<Calendar value={date} onValueChange={setDate} month={month} onMonthChange={setMonth} />

// Weeks that start on Monday, nothing in the past, nothing on a weekend
<Calendar
  weekStartsOn={1}
  min={todayString}
  isDateDisabled={(date) => [0, 6].includes(new Date(`${date}T00:00:00Z`).getUTCDay())}
/>

// A smaller calendar, without the neighbouring months' days
<Calendar size="sm" showOutsideDays={false} />

// Month and year fields, for a birth date
<Calendar captionLayout="dropdown" yearRange={[1926, 2026]} max="2026-12-31" />

// Two months side by side for a range
<Calendar mode="range" numberOfMonths={2} value={range} onValueChange={setRange} />

// A range of at least 3 days and at most 7 (counting both ends) that can't step over a booked night
<Calendar
  mode="range"
  minRangeDays={3}
  maxRangeDays={7}
  rangeSpansUnavailable={false}
  isDateDisabled={(date) => booked.has(date)}
/>

// Today and Clear buttons, and a note of your own
<Calendar showTodayButton clearable footer={<Text size="xs">Times are in your timezone.</Text>} />

// A dot for days with events, a price for others (from size lg)
<Calendar getMarker={(date) => (events.has(date) ? { tone: "info", label: "1 event" } : undefined)} />
<Calendar size="lg" getMarker={(date) => ({ content: <span>{price(date)}</span>, label: price(date) })} />

// A whole week, starting on Monday
<Calendar mode="week" weekStartsOn={1} value={week} onValueChange={setWeek} />

// The heading opens a grid of months, and that a grid of years
<Calendar captionLayout="views" />

// Any number of separate dates, at most four
<Calendar mode="multiple" maxSelected={4} value={dates} onValueChange={setDates} />

// ISO week numbers, and only the rows each month needs
<Calendar showWeekNumbers weekStartsOn={1} fixedWeeks={false} />

// Submit with a form: a date as "arrival", a range as two values under "stay[]"
<form onSubmit={handleSubmit}>
  <Calendar name="arrival" />
  <Calendar name="stay" mode="range" />
</form>

// No slide when the month changes
<Calendar animated={false} />

// Round days and month buttons
<Calendar rounded />

// A key below the calendar that explains the ring, the fills and the dimmed dates
<Calendar mode="range" showLegend isDateDisabled={isFullyBooked} />

// Fixed "today", for a test or a screenshot
<Calendar today="2026-10-14" defaultMonth="2026-10" />

// Translated, with the week starting on Monday
<Calendar
  weekStartsOn={1}
  labels={{
    calendar: "Calendario",
```
