<VisuallyHidden asChild><h2>Intro</h2></VisuallyHidden>

<ComponentHeader title="PageShell" tier="Template" />

**Which shell?** An app with no sidebar uses `PageShell`; one with a sidebar uses [`AppShell`](/docs/templates-application-shells-appshell--docs).

The page frame of an application with no sidebar: a skip link, an optional banner, your header, the page's one `<main>` (with an optional
`PageShell.Aside` beside it, in a `PageShell.Body`) and a footer. The page scrolls as a whole. It is **layout and wiring only**, and it **does not wrap your header**: put a [`Navbar`](/docs/organisms-navigation-navbar--docs) (or
any header) directly in the shell, in the order you want it on the page. The header sticks by its own means: `Navbar`'s `sticky` (with its shadow,
`hideOnScroll` and a second row of links that scrolls away), or `Affix` around a header of your own. The shell adds the page's one `<main>` landmark, an optional `complementary` aside, the
skip link's target, a footer that sits at the bottom of the window on a page too short to fill it, the page-change handling and the frame.

Given the router's page, the shell moves focus to the new page and announces it. It publishes the device's safe-area insets as `--page-shell-safe-top`
(and `-right`, `-bottom`, `-left`), so a sticky header can keep clear of a notch. On this page the live examples sit in a framed box 36rem tall, which is
wider than a phone; open a story from the sidebar on its own and narrow the window to see the phone layout, and scroll to see the header stay at the top.

### Import

```tsx
import { Navbar, PageShell } from "@dbm-system/components";
```

## Variants

### A second row of links

A `Navbar.Row` at the end of the bar with `sticky={false}` is drawn after the bar's `<header>`, so the bar sticks on its own and the row scrolls away under
it. `scroll` on its `Navbar.Links` keeps the links in the bar at every width and scrolls them sideways when they do not fit, with a fade at the edge that
has more and the current link brought into view; `rounded` and `currentVariant` give that set its own look beside the bar's. This is the `Navbar`'s, so
its Docs say the rest.

<Canvas of={PageShellStories.WithSubNav} />

### A header that hides on scroll

The `Navbar`'s own `hideOnScroll` slides a sticky bar out of view while the reader scrolls down and brings it back on the first scroll up. It stays while
focus or an open menu is inside it.

<Canvas of={PageShellStories.AutoHide} />

### A header of your own

The shell does not wrap the header, so any header works, and it sticks by its own means. A header of your own sticks by `Affix` (`asChild` puts the
sticking on your `<header>`), and the shadow once it is stuck comes from `onStickyChange`. Pad it with `--page-shell-safe-top` to keep clear of a notch.

<Canvas of={PageShellStories.CustomHeader} />

### An aside beside the page

`PageShell.Body` holds `PageShell.Main` and `PageShell.Aside` side by side, and gives them the width and padding that `PageShell.Content` gives a page
with none. The layout follows the order you write
them: an aside written first is at the start, and in right-to-left text the sides swap. Use `PageShell.Body` in place of `PageShell.Content`:
it is the page's width and padding, so don't put a `PageShell.Content` inside `Main` in a body, which would pad the page twice. Below `collapseBelow` (`lg` by default) the aside is stacked under
the page, in the same order, with no state: nothing is hidden and nothing needs a button. The aside scrolls with the page unless it is `sticky`.

<Canvas of={PageShellStories.WithAside} />

### A sticky aside

`sticky` keeps the aside in view under the header while the page scrolls. It sticks at the header's height as the shell measures it
(`--page-shell-header-height`, the first direct `<header>` while it is sticky), so a `Navbar` of any height works; pass `stickyOffset` when the header's
height changes (a second row that scrolls away, `hideOnScroll`). An aside taller than the room below the header scrolls inside itself and becomes a tab
stop while it does. Stacked on a phone it scrolls with the page.

<Canvas of={PageShellStories.StickyAside} />

### An aside as a drawer

`collapse="drawer"` takes the aside off the page below `collapseBelow` and draws its children in a `Drawer` opened from `PageShell.AsideToggle`, which you
place where you want it (the page's actions are the usual place). The open state is the root's: `asideOpen` or `defaultAsideOpen`. Growing the window past
the step closes the drawer and shows the aside again. The demo's step is `3xl` so the drawer shows in the framed box; narrow the window to see it at
`lg`.

<Canvas of={PageShellStories.AsideDrawer} />

### An aside that follows a header that hides

A sticky aside sticks under the header at the height the shell measures. A `Navbar` with `hideOnScroll` slides out of view on a scroll down and marks its
header `data-away` while it is out; the shell then publishes a height of zero, so the aside eases up to the top of the window and back down when the bar
returns (and stays down while focus is inside the bar). Nothing to pass; reduced motion jumps instead of easing. A bar with leading rows that scroll away
still needs `stickyOffset`.

<Canvas of={PageShellStories.AsideFollowsHidingHeader} />

### An aside at each end

The layout follows the order written, so one aside before `PageShell.Main` is at the start and one after it is at the end, and the sides swap in
right-to-left text. Name each: a page can have more than one `complementary` landmark. Below the step each stacks, in the order written.

<Canvas of={PageShellStories.TwoAsides} />

### Moving to another page

Press a link in the bar: its page goes to the shell as `pageKey` and `pageTitle`, which moves focus to the main area and announces the new title.

<Canvas of={PageShellStories.PageChange} />

### A page change that lands on the heading

`focusTarget="heading"` moves focus to the new page's own `h1`, instead of the whole main area, so the keyboard starts at the title (with no `h1` it is
the main area). `scrollToTopOnChange` scrolls the window to its top for a router that does not. Both are off by default.

<Canvas of={PageShellStories.PageChangeToHeading} />

### Moving between pages

`navigating` is for the time a router takes to arrive at the next page: a loading bar along the top of the window, above the header, and a polite
"Loading" announcement (`labels.loading` changes the words). `busy` on `PageShell.Main` is for a page that is reloading its own content and keeps what
is on screen.

<Canvas of={PageShellStories.Navigating} />

### A banner that can be dismissed

An `Alert` with `dismissible` and its `open` controlled by `usePersistentDismiss`: the notice stays dismissed for the rest of the session (or longer, with
`storage: "local"`) and shows nothing until the hook is ready, so a dismissed notice does not flash.

<Canvas of={PageShellStories.DismissibleBanner} />

### A page that does not exist

An `EmptyState` in the content, with the way back: its title is the page's `h1`, and the header and footer stay, so the reader is never stranded.

<Canvas of={PageShellStories.NotFoundPage} />

### Edge to edge: `Content` in three widths

`PageShell.Main` is as wide as the window, and `PageShell.Content` is one part with three ways to span, written side by side inside it:

- **Contained** (the default): the page's width limit and padding.
- **`width="full"`** (or `size="full"`): no width limit, so its content runs edge to edge. `paddingInline={0}` takes the side padding away too.
- **`bleed`**: a band as wide as `Main` (edge to edge) whose content is still kept to the page's width and padding (`width`, `size` and `paddingInline`), so
  it lines up with the rest of the page. `paddingBlock` is the band's. `className`, `style`, `id` and the `ref` are on the band, so a token in `style`
  tints it (`background: "var(--dbm-bg-canvas)"`); `text.tertiary` does not pass AA on that fill, so keep tertiary text off a tinted band. `as="section"` with an
  `aria-label` makes it a named part of the page (a `section` is a landmark only when it is named).

The story shows the three, top to bottom: the full-width section (its text at the window's edge), the `bleed` band under it (the same width, its text lined
up with the page) and the page's own `Content`. A `bleed` band and a `Content` take their `width`, `size` and `paddingInline` separately, so give both the same
ones when the page is not at the defaults.

<Canvas of={PageShellStories.FullBleedSection} />

### Content kept to a narrower width

`PageShell.Content` centres the page and limits its width on the `Container` steps; the default is `xl`. `PageShell.Body` takes the same `width`,
`size` and padding props for a page with an aside.

<Canvas of={PageShellStories.ContentWidth} />

`width` on `PageShell.Content` picks the width by what the page is: `reading` for text, `dashboard` for cards and tables, `full` for a page that fills
the window. `size` takes the steps of `Container` directly and replaces it.

### The head of a page

The head of a page (its title, breadcrumbs, description, facts and actions) is the [`PageHeader`](/docs/molecules-layout-pageheader--docs) component,
which the demo pages on this page use. The shell draws none of its own.

### The tab's title

`documentTitle` keeps the browser tab's title in step with `pageTitle`, so the router does not have to: `true` sets it to `pageTitle`, and a function
builds the whole title (`(title) => title + " · dbM"`). The title the document had comes back when the shell unmounts.

## Usage guidelines

<div className="dbm-do-dont-grid">
<Callout tone="success" title="Do">

- Put `PageShell.SkipLink` first and your header after it. The shell draws the parts in the order you write them, which is the keyboard's tab order, so
  the skip link is the first stop.
- Put the `Navbar` (or your header) directly in the shell, and let it stick by its own `sticky` (or `Affix`). Use `Navbar.SkipLink` inside a `Navbar`, and
  `PageShell.SkipLink` when the header is not a `Navbar`: both go to `#main`, which is `PageShell.Main`'s `id`.
- Name each `PageShell.Aside` with `aria-label` ("On this page", "Related"): it is a `complementary` landmark, and its name is also the title of its
  drawer.
- Put what is secondary in the aside (a table of contents, filters, related links) and write it after `PageShell.Main` when it reads second, so a
  stacked aside lands under the page. Choose `collapse="drawer"` when the aside is a tool the reader reaches for (filters, details), `stack` when it is
  content that follows the page.
- Give a second row of links its own `aria-label`, so the page's navigation landmarks are told apart from the bar's.
- Pad a sticky header with `--page-shell-safe-top` if the page draws under a notch.
- Pass `pageKey` (the router's pathname) and `pageTitle`, so a screen-reader user and a keyboard user are told about a page change that no browser
  navigation announces.
- Keep a wide table or a long code block in a component that scrolls on its own (`Table`, `CodeBlock`): the page scrolls vertically and never
  scrolls sideways.
- Use `BackToTop` on a long page, and pass it the box that scrolls (`scrollContainerRef`) when the shell is drawn inside one.
- Give a page with nothing to show its own `EmptyState`, and a failed one `tone="danger"` with `announce`.

</Callout>
<Callout tone="danger" title="Don't">

- Don't use `PageShell` for an app with a sidebar: [`AppShell`](/docs/templates-application-shells-appshell--docs) has the sidebar, the rail and
  the aside. Don't use it for a page with no header of its own either: a plain
  [`Container`](/docs/atoms-layout-container--docs) with `as="main"` is the page there.
- Don't put a second `<main>` inside it, and don't wrap a `Navbar` in a `<header>` or a box of your own: a `Navbar` draws its own `<header>`, and a box
  around it is the box it sticks within, so it stops following the window.
- Don't set `overflow` on an element around the shell, or give it a height: it makes that element the box the header sticks to, so the header stops
  following the window. The shell is as tall as the window already (and taller when the page is).
- Don't put ids, or content that keeps its own state (a form), in an aside with `collapse="drawer"`: its children are drawn twice while the drawer is open, once
  in the hidden column and once in the drawer.
- Don't put the page's primary content in a section beside `Body`: it is outside `<main>`, so the skip link skips it. Keep sections beside `Body` to supplementary
  content such as a hero band or a call to action.
- Don't put the page's primary content in the aside: on a phone it is under the page, or behind a button.
- Don't use `sticky` on a tall aside and expect it to be read without scrolling: it scrolls inside itself, so keep it short or keep it a plain block.
- Don't keep a tall banner above the header and expect it to stay: it scrolls away with the page.
- Don't put important navigation only in a second row of links on a phone: it scrolls sideways, so keep the few places everyone needs in the bar's menu.

</Callout>
</div>

## Best practices

- Use `Content` when there is no aside and `Body` when there is. Use both only for a deliberately narrower column inside a `Body`.
- Choose `size` on `PageShell.Content` (or on `PageShell.Body` for a page with an aside) for the page's reading width: `xl` (the default) suits a page of cards, `md` suits a form or an article. A
  band that runs edge to edge is `PageShell.Content` with `bleed`.
- Let the footer be small. It holds the links people look for at the end of a page (help, privacy, terms), and it sits at the bottom of the window on a
  short page, so it is always reachable.
- Printing prints the page, not the chrome: the skip link and the loading bar are left out, and the banner, main and footer are ordinary rows. The header
  prints as it does alone. Put your own page breaks in the page's own CSS.
- Use `navigating` for the gap between pages and `busy` for a page reloading its own content: the first is the window's, the second is the main area's.
- Name each `Navbar.Links`, so a screen reader's list of navigation landmarks tells them apart.
- Use `busy` for a page that is reloading its content: it keeps what is on screen and says so, where an `EmptyState` replaces it.
## Accessibility

<Callout tone="info" title="Who provides which landmark">

The shell supplies `PageShell.Main` (the one `main`), `PageShell.Aside` (a `complementary`, when there is one) and `PageShell.Footer` (the `contentinfo`). The banner `<header>` comes from your header: the `Navbar`
draws its own, and a header of your own is a `<header>`. Each `Navbar.Links` is a named `navigation`; a second row's `<nav>` is a landmark outside the
banner. All of them are real elements, not roles on divs. The shell wraps no header, so a `Navbar` is never wrapped in a second banner.

</Callout>

| Key | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `Tab` | Moves through the skip link, then the header, then the main area's content, then the footer, in markup order. |
| `Tab` into a hidden header | Slides the header back into view (the `Navbar`'s `hideOnScroll`). |
| `Enter` on a skip link | Moves focus to `PageShell.Main`, past the header. |
| `Tab` into a sticky aside taller than the window | The aside is a tab stop while it scrolls inside itself, with a focus ring; the arrow keys and Page Up and Down scroll it. |
| `Enter` on the aside's toggle | Opens the aside's drawer and moves focus into it. |
| `Escape` in the aside's drawer | Closes it and returns focus to the toggle. |
| `Escape` in the bar's menu panel | Closes it and returns focus to the menu button (the `Navbar`'s own behaviour below its `collapseBelow` width). |

- `PageShell.Main` takes focus from the skip link and when the page changes (`tabindex="-1"`). A focus ring is drawn just inside its edge whenever
  the keyboard has focused it. The page scrolls as a whole, so the main area is not itself a tab stop.
- When `pageKey` changes, focus moves to `PageShell.Main` and, with `pageTitle`, the new title is announced politely. Neither happens on the first
  render, so the page's own load is not announced twice, and nothing happens while `pageKey` is left out. The shell does not scroll the window: that
  is the router's.
- `PageShell.Aside` is an `<aside>`, a `complementary` landmark, and is named. A development warning says so when it has no `aria-label` or
  `aria-labelledby`. A sticky aside that scrolls inside itself is a tab stop (`tabindex="0"`) so the keyboard can scroll it, with a focus ring.
- `PageShell.AsideToggle` shows only below the aside's collapse step with `collapse="drawer"`. It has `aria-haspopup="dialog"` and `aria-expanded`. The
  drawer is a dialog named by the aside's `aria-label` (else `labels.title`): focus moves into it, `Escape` or a press outside closes it and focus
  returns to the toggle. Growing the window past the step closes it.
- With `focusTarget="heading"` focus goes to the first `h1` in the main area instead (made focusable only for that moment, and not a tab stop afterwards),
  and to the main area when there is none. `scrollToTopOnChange` scrolls the window to its top; it does not move focus.
- While `navigating` is set a polite status region says "Loading" (the words are `labels.loading`), and clears when it ends; the bar is decoration and
  hidden from assistive technology. It is not drawn on paper.
- While `busy` is set the main area has `aria-busy="true"` and a polite status region says "Loading"; when it clears the region empties, so nothing more
  is read out. The bar is decoration and hidden from assistive technology.
- The sticky header and the page's own sticky parts keep the system's order of layers (the page's content and `Affix` on the `sticky` step, the skip
  link on the `fixed` step, and every menu, popover and dialog above those).
- A notch or a home indicator never covers the shell: the frame keeps the sides and the bottom clear, the skip link is drawn below the top inset, and a
  sticky header pads itself with the published `--page-shell-safe-top`.
- The shell adds no colour pairing of its own: the page background is the surface colour, and text inside uses the primary text colour (at least 4.5:1
  on it in every theme).
- The reading direction is the shell's (`dir`, else the nearest `DirectionProvider`'s), and the `Navbar` inside takes it from the shell. Set `lang` beside it.
