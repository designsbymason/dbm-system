<VisuallyHidden asChild><h2>Intro</h2></VisuallyHidden>

<ComponentHeader title="Navbar" tier="Organism" />

The application header: a named banner at the top of the page holding the brand, the primary navigation
and a place for the actions that belong to the whole app (search, an account menu, a call to action).
Below a width you choose, the navigation leaves the bar and is offered in a side panel behind a menu
button, so a phone gets a list it can reach with a thumb. A compound component, meaning it is made of
named sub-parts you compose together: `Navbar.Row`s (optional bands of the one header), `Navbar.Brand`, `Navbar.Links`
(several are allowed) holding `Navbar.Link` items and `Navbar.Group`s of them, `Navbar.Search`, `Navbar.Actions` holding `Navbar.Action`s, `Navbar.PanelActions` for what the menu panel shows
at its foot, `Navbar.SkipLink`, and `Navbar.MenuButton`, which is drawn for you whenever there are links.

### Import

```tsx
import { Navbar } from "@dbm-system/components";
```

## Properties

`Navbar` is the `<header>` element, so it also accepts any other native `<header>` attribute beyond
what's listed here (e.g. `onFocus`, `title`), passed straight through. `dir` is set by the bar and can't
be replaced by a same-named attribute. The bar finds `Navbar.Links`, `Navbar.MenuButton` and `Navbar.PanelActions` among its children and
in its `Navbar.Row`s, looking through fragments, to draw the menu panel and its button; one wrapped in another component still draws,
but isn't noticed.

### Navbar.Row properties

One band of the bar. Every row starts from the bar's `variant`, `size`, `linkSize` and `contained` and can set its own. Also accepts any other
native `<div>` attribute beyond what's listed here. A bar with no rows draws its parts as one row, so a single-row bar needs none.

### Navbar.Brand properties

The logo and name, linking home. Also accepts any other native `<a>` attribute beyond what's listed
here (e.g. `onClick`, `title`), passed straight through.

### Navbar.Links properties

The primary navigation: a `<nav>` holding a list of `Navbar.Link`s. Also accepts any other native

<PropertiesTable of={NavbarLinksStories} order={linksPropOrder} />

### Navbar.Link properties

One link of the navigation, an `<li>` holding the `<a>`. Everything below, and any other native `<a>`
attribute beyond what's listed here, goes on the `<a>`.

### Navbar.Group properties

A link that opens more links: a dropdown in the bar, an expandable group in the menu panel. Also accepts any

<PropertiesTable of={NavbarGroupStories} order={groupPropOrder} />

The bar's search field, in a `role="search"` landmark. It also accepts any other `SearchInput` prop (`onSearch`, `debounceMs`,

<PropertiesTable of={NavbarSearchStories} order={searchPropOrder} />

### Navbar.Action properties

One action in the bar's end that can leave the bar below a width (`hideBelow`). Also accepts any other
native `<div>` attribute beyond what's listed here. A bare child of `Navbar.Actions` stays at every width;

<PropertiesTable of={NavbarActionStories} order={actionPropOrder} />

What the menu panel shows at its foot, drawn only there. Put it among the bar's children or in a row, in a fragment if you like (a part

`<div>` attribute beyond what's listed here.

A link that jumps past the bar, hidden until it takes keyboard focus. Also accepts any other native `<a>`
attribute beyond what's listed here (e.g. `onClick`, `title`), passed straight through.

The bar's end. Also accepts any other native `<div>` attribute beyond what's listed here.

## Variants

### Variants

`outlined` is a surface with a line under it, `filled` the same surface with no line, and `ghost` nothing of its own,
for a bar that sits over a hero or a coloured page. `frosted` is a translucent surface (90% of `bg.surface`) that blurs what is behind it,
with a line under it: a sticky bar over a page, a hero or an image. It stays mostly opaque so the text keeps 4.5:1 over any content,
and is a plain surface where `backdrop-filter` isn't supported and in forced colours.

#### Changing the background

There is no prop for the fill, because each `Navbar.Row` draws its own, not the `<header>` behind the rows. To use another colour, put a
`style` on the row (an inline style beats the variant's class), on a token so it follows the theme:

```tsx
<Navbar>
  <Navbar.Row style={{ backgroundColor: "var(--dbm-bg-brand-subtle)" }}>…</Navbar.Row>
</Navbar>
```

A `style` on `Navbar` itself shows only with `variant="ghost"`, whose rows are transparent (and a stuck ghost bar keeps it, since an
inline style outranks the surface it would otherwise take). A bar written without a `Navbar.Row` has no row to reach, so use `ghost` and
the bar's `style`, or add the row. The links' colours are checked against the standard surfaces, so check the contrast of any other fill.

### A header of several rows

A `Navbar.Row` is a band of the one `<header>`, and each can set its own `variant`, `size` and `contained`. Here: a thin courtesy strip
(`size="xs"`, with `quiet` links); a taller row (`size="lg"`) with the logo, a smaller search field (`size="sm"`), centred between the logo and the buttons, and medium, neutral, tertiary icon buttons with a caption under each on a wide window (`label` and `hideLabelBelow` on `IconButton`);
and a smaller row (`size="sm"`) of primary links, centred (`align="center"`), in bold capitals (a `style` on `Navbar.Links` sets the text of every
link in it), with none marked current to begin with (a home page); choosing one makes it current, as a router would. Give each `Navbar.Links` its own name, since they are separate landmarks.

On a phone the same header lays itself out differently: the logo row is drawn in three columns (`columns="balanced"` on the `Navbar.Row`), with the menu
button and the search button at the start, the logo in the centre and the other buttons at the end. The logo is central however many buttons are on each side. `Navbar.MenuButton` takes a `variant`, a `tone` and a `size` and `Navbar.Search` a `buttonVariant`, a `buttonTone` and a `buttonSize`
so they can match the icon buttons beside them. Narrow the window to see it.

`columns` gathers the row's parts into a group for the start, the centre and the end, and below `columnsBelow` (`"md"` unless set) draws the three as
a grid. `"balanced"` gives the two sides equal room, so the logo is central whatever is on each side; a track list such as `columns="auto 1fr auto"` sets
the widths yourself. Each part is in the column it belongs in (the menu button and the search at the start, the brand and the links in the centre, the actions
at the end) unless its `column` says otherwise. Above the step nothing changes: the parts flow in the row as without `columns`.

The page has the groups in the order their first part is written, and the parts of each in the order written, so write the brand first to keep it first in the page.
The menu button is drawn first in its column wherever it is written. A side with more in it than its track holds spills over its neighbour, so give it a wider track.

### Several sets of links in the menu panel

Below the collapse width, every `Navbar.Links` puts its links into the one menu panel, in the order written, with a line between the sets.
The `quiet` ones, a courtesy row's, come last. Without a `Navbar.MenuButton` of your own, the button goes at the end of the row with the brand; here it is placed first,
beside the search button. A row that held only links goes with them, so the bar is not left with empty bands. This demo collapses below the widest step so the panel shows on any window narrower than 1920px. Its icon buttons keep the default brand tone (the rows story greys them with `tone="neutral"`), and choosing a primary link makes it current, in the panel when it is reopened as it would be after moving to another page.

### Search as a sheet

`Navbar.Search` takes the room between its neighbours. Below its `collapseBelow` width the field leaves the bar for a button that
opens it in a sheet from the top of the screen, so a phone isn't left with a squeezed field and the tab order stays the one the bar
is written in. Press Enter in the sheet's field and it closes. Here the bar is the logo row alone, with the links in it, centred between the logo and the buttons (`align="center"`), and choosing one makes it current. The search button is last in the same group as the account and cart buttons, so it is the one at the end, and all three are in the brand tone. Below the collapse width the links go into the menu panel and the row is drawn in columns: the menu button at the start, the logo in the centre, the buttons at the end. The columns are `auto 1fr auto`, not `balanced`: three buttons are wider than a phone's equal side column, so the end takes the room it needs and the logo is centred in what is left.

### A strip that scrolls away

In a sticky bar, a leading row with `sticky={false}` scrolls away while the rest stays at the top. Only rows at the start or the end can: the bar sticks
as one box around the rows between, so a row that scrolls away can't sit between two that stick. The icon buttons have no captions here, and choosing a primary link makes it current, as if each were another page.

<Canvas of={NavbarStories.RowsScrollAway} />

### A second row of links that scrolls away

A row at the end with `sticky={false}` is drawn after the bar's `<header>`, so the bar sticks on its own and the row scrolls away under it. Its `<nav>` is a
navigation landmark outside the banner, where a sub-navigation belongs. `scroll` on its `Navbar.Links` keeps the links in the bar at every width instead of
moving them into the menu (they are not copied into the panel, the row stays on a phone, and a bar of only such sets draws no menu button). The set scrolls
sideways when the links do not fit, with no scrollbar, fades at the edge that has more, and scrolls the current link into view when it changes; the links start
at the start of the row, and `align="center"` would centre them while everything fits. `currentVariant` and `rounded` on the set give it a look of its own beside the bar's, here round and subtle pills under a bar
that underlines. `variant="filled"` on the row draws no line under it. Put only `Navbar.Link`s in a scrolling set: a `Navbar.Group`'s floating list would be cut.
Narrow the window to see the row scroll.

<Canvas of={NavbarStories.SectionsRow} />

### Link size

`size` is the bar's height. `linkSize` sets the links' text size, icon size and padding on its own, on the same five steps as `Menu`'s `size`,
so a tall bar can have small links and the reverse. Left out it follows `size` (`xs`, `sm`, `md` and `lg` read the step of the same name), so a taller bar has larger links.
Set it on the bar, on a `Navbar.Row` or on one `Navbar.Links`; the nearest wins. A `quiet` set reads one step below its size, and a
`Navbar.Group`'s dropdown takes the same step for its items. The menu panel's links keep their own size, and the bar's height is a
floor that a taller link still grows.

<Canvas of={NavbarStories.LinkSizes} />

A link's `icon`, its trailing arrow and its `badge` follow the link size too: the arrow is a step behind the icon. The badge does not: it is
`Badge`'s solid brand look as a round 20px at every link size.

The links' weight and case are not props: a link takes its text style from its set, so one `style` on `Navbar.Links` sets them for the
whole set, on the tokens:

```tsx
<Navbar.Links style={{ fontWeight: "var(--dbm-font-weight-bold)", textTransform: "uppercase" }}>…</Navbar.Links>
```

The menu panel draws its own copy of the links and keeps its own text, so this styles the bar's links only.

### Marking the current page

`currentVariant` chooses how the current link is marked: `underline` (the default) is a brand line under the link
(a bar at the start edge in the phone panel), `subtle` a tinted pill, and `solid` a brand fill. It is one look for the
whole bar, the phone panel and a `Navbar.Group` that holds the page included, and can't be set per link.
`aria-current="page"` is set whichever it is, and a link is never told apart by colour alone. The underline grows in when a link
becomes the current one, and only then: not on a first paint, and not when the menu panel opens on a link already current.

<Canvas of={NavbarStories.CurrentVariants} />

### Actions that move into the menu panel

Only the links leave the bar by themselves. Wrap an action in `Navbar.Action` with `hideBelow` to take it out
of the bar below a width, and give `Navbar.PanelActions` the same control to bring it back at the foot of the
menu panel, where a thumb can reach it. This demo hides its button below the widest step.

### A skip link

`Navbar.SkipLink` is the first thing `Tab` reaches: a link that jumps past the bar to the page's main
content, out of sight until it takes focus. Put it first in the bar and the bar first on the page, and give
the main element `tabIndex={-1}` so focus lands in it.

<Canvas of={NavbarStories.SkipLink} />

### Icons, counts and external links

A link takes an `icon` before its text, a `badge` after it (a count is read as part of the link's name) and
is marked `external` when its `href` starts `http://`, `https://` or `//`: it opens in a new tab with a trailing
icon and a hidden "(opens in a new tab)".

A `badge` that is plain text or a number is drawn in `Badge`'s solid brand look, as a round 20px (its subtle look on a current link drawn as a brand fill, where a solid pill would vanish). To choose its tone
or variant, pass a `Badge` yourself (`badge={<Badge tone="danger" variant="solid">2</Badge>}`): it is drawn as written, with the link's size
where it sets none, and a margin that keeps the link as tall as a plain one. Pass the `Badge` element itself, not a component that returns one, or it is taken for
content and drawn inside the link's own.

### Transparent until it sticks

A sticky `ghost` bar has no surface over the top of the page (a hero, say) and takes one, with a shadow,

### Hides on scroll

`hideOnScroll` slides a sticky bar out of view while the reader scrolls down and brings it back on a scroll up
or near the top, so it costs no room while reading. It stays while anything in it has keyboard focus.
While the bar is slid out its `<header>` carries a `data-away` attribute, which a page frame can read: `PageShell` uses it to let a sticky aside follow
the bar up and back down.

<Canvas of={NavbarStories.HidesOnScroll} />

### Rounded links

`rounded` makes each link and group button a pill, as it does on `Button`, `IconButton` and `Toolbar`. It is the
links that round (and the focus ring and the current link's tint or fill with them); the bar keeps its shape.
It reads best with `subtle` or `solid`, since an underline has no shape to round.

<Canvas of={NavbarStories.Rounded} />

### Sizes

The bar is as tall as an `IconButton` of the same step plus room above and below, so a button in the bar
never touches its edges. The step is also the default size of the links (`linkSize` sizes them apart), of the menu and search
buttons, and of any `Button` or `IconButton` in `Navbar.Actions` that sets no `size` of its own; a search field in the actions
keeps the size you give it. `xs` is a thin strip, for a courtesy row.

## Usage guidelines

<div className="dbm-do-dont-grid">
<Callout tone="success" title="Do">

- When a page offers the same links somewhere else on a phone (in a `Sidebar`'s menu panel, say), hide the set with `hideBelow` and set the bar's
  `collapseBelow` to `"never"`: the bar then has no menu button of its own, and the page keeps one.
- Use one `Navbar` per page, at the top, for the links a person needs on every screen.
- Keep the primary links few (five or six) and put the rest in a `Menu` or a footer.
- Mark the current page with `current`, from your router, so it is right on a server-rendered page.
- Put app-wide controls (search, notifications, the account menu) in `Navbar.Actions`.
- Use `sticky` for an app people scroll inside; leave it off for a short marketing page.
- Pass the navigation a name when the page has more than one (`aria-label` on `Navbar.Links`).
- Use `asChild` with your router's link so navigation stays client-side.

</Callout>
<Callout tone="danger" title="Don't">

- Don't use a `Navbar` for the actions of an editor or a table: that is a `Toolbar`.
- Don't use it for a persistent navigation column: that is a `Sidebar`.
- Don't give `Navbar.Link` an `id`: the same items are drawn in the bar and in the menu panel.
- Don't put more than one `Navbar` on a page unless each has its own `aria-label`; they are all banner
  landmarks.
- Don't hide the menu button or set `collapseBelow` so low that a phone shows a bar of links that does not
  fit.
- Don't fill the bar with controls that need the arrow keys (a text field with a menu inside): it is a row
  of ordinary tab stops, not a toolbar.

</Callout>
</div>

## Best practices

- The bar is a `<header>` (a banner landmark) holding a `<nav>`. Its skip link, `Navbar.SkipLink`, goes first in the bar and
  points at the `main` landmark, which the bar doesn't own (see the examples below).
- Links are ordinary tab stops, in reading order: brand, links, actions, then the menu button.
- The collapse follows the window width, so a narrow window on a desktop gets the same menu a phone does.
  If the panel is open when the window grows past the step, it closes.
- A link that opens more links is a `Navbar.Group`: a `Menu` in the bar and an expandable group in the panel. Keep a group to a handful of
  links; a wide panel of grouped links, with descriptions or images, is a mega-menu, which is `NavigationMenu`.
- `menuSide="start"` is the right edge in right-to-left text. Pass `dir` on the bar and it reaches the
  panel too, which is drawn in a portal and would otherwise follow the page.
- Use `labels` to translate the menu button, the navigation's name, the panel's close button, the skip link, the external-link cue and the search field's name.
- Rows are for a header with more than one band: the courtesy strip scrolls away when the bar is sticky (`sticky={false}`), the rest sticks as one, and
  the automatic menu button goes at the end of the row with the brand. Put every part in a row once there are rows.
- Only the links collapse by themselves. A bar with a search field, an account menu and a button can overflow a phone: wrap the ones that
  should leave in `Navbar.Action hideBelow` and repeat them in `Navbar.PanelActions`.
- Put a skip link first in the bar and the bar first on the page; the main element needs `tabIndex={-1}`.
- `hideOnScroll` reads the scroll position from a passive listener, one read per frame, and never hides a bar that has focus; in a scrolling box
  of your own name it with `scrollContainerRef`.

## Accessibility

<Callout tone="info" title="A banner landmark holding a named navigation, with the current page marked">

- The root is a `<header>`, which is a banner landmark when it is not inside an article or section.
  `aria-label` names it when a page has another header.
- `Navbar.Links` is a `<nav>` with a name (`labels.navigation`, "Main", unless you pass `aria-label`)
  holding a `role="list"` list; each link is a `role="listitem"`, which keeps list semantics under
  Safari with VoiceOver, where a list without markers loses them.
- `Navbar.SkipLink` is a real link, the first stop of the page when it is first in the bar: out of sight (clipped to a pixel, not `display: none`, so
  it can take focus) until it has keyboard focus, when it is drawn over the page's top corner with a visible edge and the standard focus ring.
- `Navbar.PanelActions` puts its children in the menu panel's footer, inside the same modal dialog, so they are in its focus loop and read
  after the links. A control hidden from the bar by `hideBelow` is `display: none`, so it leaves the tab order and the accessibility tree there.
- An external link's new tab is announced: a visually hidden "(opens in a new tab)" follows its text, and a count in `badge` is part of the name.
- `hideOnScroll` never hides a bar that has keyboard focus (focus inside it shows it), and a bar that is hidden still takes focus when `Tab` reaches it,
  which brings it back.
- The current page is `aria-current="page"`. A disabled link is `aria-disabled` and stays focusable, with
  its click blocked.
- Keys: `Tab` and `Shift+Tab` move through the brand, the links, the actions and the menu button in order;
  there are no arrow-key shortcuts, because this is a navigation and not a toolbar. `Enter` follows a
  link.
- `Navbar.Search` is a `role="search"` landmark named by `labels.search` or its own `aria-label`; below its step it is a button with that name, and
  the sheet it opens is a modal dialog with the field focused, closed by `Escape` or `Enter`, returning focus to the button.
- Every `Navbar.Links` is its own `<nav>`, so give each a different name (a development warning says when two match); in the menu panel they are
  separate named navigations in the order written, the `quiet` ones last, and rows are plain bands with no role of their own, so the page has one banner.
- A `Navbar.Group` in the bar is a button with `aria-haspopup="menu"` and `aria-expanded` that opens a menu: `Enter`, `Space` and
  `ArrowDown` open it, the arrow keys and typeahead move through the links, `Escape` closes it and focus returns to the button. In the
  menu panel it is a disclosure button with `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls` pointing at its list of links, which are out of the tab
  order and the accessibility tree while it is collapsed.
- The menu button is an `IconButton` named "Menu" with `aria-expanded` and `aria-haspopup="dialog"`. The
  panel is a modal dialog named "Menu" with focus held inside; `Escape`, the scrim and the close button
  close it, and focus returns to the menu button.
- Below the collapse width the bar's own links are `display: none`, so they leave the tab order and the
  accessibility tree; the panel's copy is what a screen reader finds.
- Every control is at least 24 by 24 pixels. The focus ring is the standard two-pixel outline in
  `border.focus`.
- The motion is a short fade of the tint on hover (the text and icon keep their colour, as a menu item's do), and the underline growing in when a link becomes the current one (from the link's start edge in the bar, from its top in the menu panel), both removed under `prefers-reduced-motion`, where the line is simply there. In
  forced-colours mode a filled or stuck bar and the current link get a one-pixel outline, since a fill or
  shadow alone would vanish.
- `subtle` uses `text.brand` on `bg.brand-subtle`, the pairing `Tabs` uses; `solid` uses `text.on-brand` on `bg.brand`, the pairing
  a primary `Button` uses, with its focus ring drawn inside the link in `icon.on-brand`; `underline` keeps `text.primary` and draws a
  `bg.brand` line. In forced colours the underline is a system-colour line and the other two get an outline.

</Callout>

  <RouterLink to="/products">Products</RouterLink>

  <Navbar.Link href="/products">Products</Navbar.Link>

    <Navbar.Link href="/guides" current={path === "/guides"}>Guides</Navbar.Link>
  </Navbar.Group>
</Navbar.Links>

<Navbar.Link href="/inbox" icon={BellIcon} badge="3">Inbox</Navbar.Link>
<Navbar.Link href="https://status.example.com">Status</Navbar.Link>

{/* A button drawn as a link: it leaves the phone panel open, where a link closes it */}
<Navbar.Link asChild>
  <button type="button" onClick={openSearch}>Search</button>
</Navbar.Link>

{/* An action that leaves the bar on a phone and comes back at the foot of the menu panel */}
<Navbar>
  <Navbar.Links>…</Navbar.Links>
  <Navbar.Actions>
    <Navbar.Action hideBelow="md">
      <Button size="sm">Sign in</Button>
    </Navbar.Action>
  </Navbar.Actions>
  <Navbar.PanelActions>
    <Button>Sign in</Button>
  </Navbar.PanelActions>
</Navbar>

{/* Links centred between the brand and the actions */}
<Navbar.Links align="center">…</Navbar.Links>

{/* A header of several rows: a thin strip that scrolls away, a taller logo row (on a phone, in columns: menu and search buttons, a centred logo, the other
    buttons), and a small row of links, centred, in capitals, none current to begin with (a home page); choosing one makes it current (page is yours) */}
<Navbar sticky>
  <Navbar.Row size="xs" variant="outlined" sticky={false}>
    <Navbar.Links aria-label="Help" quiet align="end">
      <Navbar.Link href="/help">Help</Navbar.Link>

    </Navbar.Links>
  </Navbar.Row>

    <Navbar.Actions style={{ marginInlineStart: 0 }}>
      <IconButton icon={ShoppingCartIcon} aria-label="Cart, 2 items" variant="tertiary" tone="neutral" size="md" label="Cart" hideLabelBelow="md" />
    </Navbar.Actions>
    <Navbar.MenuButton variant="tertiary" tone="neutral" size="md" />
  </Navbar.Row>
  <Navbar.Row size="sm" variant="outlined">
    <Navbar.Links
      aria-label="Categories"
      align="center"
      style={{ fontWeight: "var(--dbm-font-weight-bold)", textTransform: "uppercase" }}
    >
      <Navbar.Link href="/women" current={page === "women"}>Women</Navbar.Link>
      <Navbar.Link href="/men" current={page === "men"}>Men</Navbar.Link>
    </Navbar.Links>
  </Navbar.Row>
</Navbar>

{/* The search field below its step is a button that opens a sheet; keep the two fields in step with value and onChange */}
<Navbar.Search collapseBelow="lg" value={query} onChange={(event) => setQuery(event.target.value)} />

{/* Transparent over a hero until it sticks, and out of the way while reading */}
<Navbar sticky variant="ghost" hideOnScroll>
  …
</Navbar>

<Navbar

  onMenuOpenChange={(next, { reason }) => {

  }}
  onMenuOpenChangeComplete={(isOpen) => !isOpen && resetSearch()}
>
  …
</Navbar>

<Navbar labels={{ menu: "Menu", navigation: "Main", close: "Close", skipLink: "Skip to content", externalLink: "(opens in a new tab)", search: "Search" }}>
  …
</Navbar>

{/* A native <header> attribute, passed straight through */}
<Navbar aria-label="Site header" title="dbM">
  …
</Navbar>
